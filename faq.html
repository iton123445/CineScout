FAQ & Copyright

Quick answers about how CineScout works, plus how to reach us about copyright.

The basics

Is CineScout free?

Yes. CineScout is completely free, has no ads and doesn't require an account to browse. A free account lets you save movies to My List, rate them and comment under your name.

Does CineScout host or stream copyrighted movies?

No. CineScout doesn't host, upload or distribute copyrighted films or shows. It shows movie information from TMDB and sends you to official services such as Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+ or Apple TV to watch. The only full films that play on CineScout are public-domain titles from the Internet Archive.

Where does the movie information come from?

Titles, posters, cast, ratings and trailers links come from The Movie Database (TMDB). Streaming availability is provided by JustWatch through TMDB. Trailers play from YouTube. CineScout uses the TMDB API but isn't endorsed or certified by TMDB.

Is CineScout affiliated with Netflix, Prime Video or other services?

No. We're not affiliated with, endorsed by or paid by any streaming service, studio or distributor. Buttons simply open a search for the title on that service's own website. All trademarks and logos belong to their owners.

Watching

Why does a movie say it isn't available in my country?

Streaming rights differ by country. Each movie's Where to watch section uses the country shown next to it, which is detected from your browser. Pick another country to compare, and check back later because catalogs change often.

The streaming info looks wrong or outdated.

Availability comes from third parties and can lag behind real changes. Always confirm on the service itself before subscribing, renting or buying.

What's the difference between “Free” and “Free · ads”?

Free movies are public-domain classics that play right here on CineScout. Free · ads movies stream free, usually with ads, on official services in your country such as Tubi, Plex, The Roku Channel, Pluto TV or YouTube. CineScout links you to that service; it doesn't stream those films itself. Turn on Free to watch in the filters to browse them. Kanopy and Hoopla are also free, with a public library card.

How are the “Free” movies legal?

Films marked Free are in the public domain or openly licensed, often because their copyright expired or was never renewed. Examples include Night of the Living Dead (1968), Nosferatu (1922) and His Girl Friday (1940). They play from the Internet Archive's official player. Public-domain status can differ by country, so please follow your local laws.

A trailer or free film won't play.

Videos are played by YouTube or the Internet Archive. Ad blockers, privacy extensions or a slow connection can stop them from loading, so try disabling the extension for this site or refreshing the page.

Your account

How do I change my display name or password?

Sign in, click your initial in the top bar and choose Account settings. Changing your name also updates it on your older comments.

How do I delete my account?

Go to Account settings → Delete account. This permanently erases your account, My List, ratings and comments immediately.

I forgot my password.

Click Sign in → Forgot password? and we'll email you a reset link. Check your spam folder if it doesn't arrive within a few minutes.

How do I report a comment?

Click Report next to any comment. Reports are private, and we remove comments that break the Terms of Use.

Copyright & takedown requests

CineScout respects intellectual property. We don't host copyrighted films. Images and information come from TMDB, trailers from YouTube, and free films only from public-domain sources. If you're a rights holder and believe something shown on CineScout infringes your rights (for example an image, a comment, or a film you believe isn't public domain), please send a request with:

  1. The work you believe is infringed.
  2. The exact page or item on CineScout (a link or the movie title and where it appears).
  3. Your name and how we can contact you.
  4. A statement that you have a good-faith belief the use isn't authorized, and that your information is accurate and you are the rights holder or authorized to act for them.

Send it by opening an issue on our GitHub page. We review requests promptly and remove or disable material that infringes. For content hosted by TMDB, YouTube or the Internet Archive, you can also contact those services directly, since removing it at the source removes it everywhere.
